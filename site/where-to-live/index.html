Where to live

Where to live in Panama

Expats in Panama mostly settle in nine regions. Panama City offers convenience and healthcare; the Pacific beaches around Coronado offer easy access to the city; Boquete and Volcán offer a cool mountain climate; the Azuero Peninsula offers authentic small-town life and surf; and Bocas del Toro offers Caribbean island living. The right choice depends mostly on climate, how close you want to be to a major hospital, and how much you want an established expat community.

How to choose

  • Climate first. If you dislike heat, start with Boquete, Volcán or El Valle.
  • Healthcare distance. Map the drive to the nearest hospital you'd actually use.
  • Community. Boquete, Coronado and Panama City have large expat communities; much of Azuero and Veraguas does not, and Spanish matters more there.
  • Season. Visit in the rainy season (roughly May to November in most of the country) as well as the dry season.

Common questions

Where do most expats live in Panama?

The largest expat communities are in Panama City, the Pacific beach towns around Coronado and the Chiriquí highlands around Boquete. Smaller communities live on the Azuero Peninsula, in Bocas del Toro and in El Valle de Antón.

Where is the coolest climate in Panama?

The Chiriquí highlands, including Boquete, Volcán and Cerro Punta, have spring-like days and cool nights. El Valle de Antón is also noticeably cooler than the coast.

What is the best beach town in Panama for expats?

Coronado is the most established, with easy access to Panama City. Pedasí and the Azuero coast suit people who want a quieter, more traditional town, and Bocas del Toro suits those who want Caribbean island life.
