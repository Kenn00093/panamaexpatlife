Getting connected

Phones and internet in Panama

For a scouting trip, a travel eSIM gives you data the moment you land. Once you're living here, a local prepaid or postpaid plan from Panama's two mobile carriers, Tigo and +Móvil, is cheaper. In cities you can get fibre or cable internet at home; in rural areas, Starlink is an option.

Scouting trip: a travel eSIM

An eSIM is set up on your phone before you leave, so you have maps, ride-hailing and WhatsApp as soon as you land, without hunting for a SIM shop. Check that your phone is unlocked and supports eSIM, buy a Panama plan from a provider such as Airalo or Saily (affiliate links) before you fly, and switch it on when you arrive. Keep your home SIM active for bank security codes sent by text.

Living here: a local SIM

Panama has two mobile carriers, Tigo and +Móvil (Cable & Wireless). Both sell cheap prepaid plans and monthly contracts. Coverage is good in cities and towns but patchy in the mountains, on remote beaches and on some islands, so ask neighbours which network works best where you plan to live. Bring your passport when you buy a SIM.

+Móvil has begun offering satellite messaging on some phones in areas with no signal. It's limited to messaging and a few apps for now, but useful in remote places.

WhatsApp runs everything

Almost everyone in Panama, from landlords and lawyers to doctors and the local hardware store, uses WhatsApp. Set it up before you arrive and keep the same number if you can, so your contacts don't change when you switch SIMs.

Home internet

  • Cities and larger towns generally have fibre or cable internet from the same two companies, fast enough for video calls and streaming.
  • Rural areas may have only slow wireless service or none at all. Starlink satellite internet is available in Panama and is a common fix where nothing else reaches.
  • Test before you sign a lease. If you work online, run a speed test at the property at a busy time of day, and ask what happens during power cuts.

Common questions

Which mobile carrier is best in Panama?

Panama has two carriers, Tigo and +Móvil. Both cover cities and towns well; in rural areas coverage varies, so ask locals which network works best where you'll be.

Should I get an eSIM or a local SIM for a trip to Panama?

For a short trip, a travel eSIM is the easiest option because it works the moment you land. For longer stays, a local prepaid plan is usually cheaper.

Can I get good internet in rural Panama?

Sometimes. Towns often have fibre or cable; more remote homes may rely on Starlink satellite internet. Test the connection before signing a lease if you work online.
