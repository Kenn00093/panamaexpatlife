Cost of living

The real cost of living in Panama

Panama is cheaper than most of North America and Western Europe, but not as cheap as its reputation. Your biggest variables are where you live, how much you use air conditioning, and whether you shop like a local or buy imported brands. A household that rents outside Panama City, eats local produce and uses little air conditioning spends far less than one living in a city tower on imported groceries.

What drives your monthly costs

Main cost drivers for expats in Panama
CostTends to be cheaper when…Tends to be pricier when…
HousingYou live outside Panama City and rent long-term from a local ownerYou want a furnished ocean-view apartment in the city or a gated beach community
ElectricityYou live in the cooler highlands, where few homes need air conditioningYou run air conditioning day and night on the coast or in the city
GroceriesYou buy local fruit, vegetables, rice, chicken and fishYou buy imported brands, wine and cheese
HealthcareYou use local private clinics and local insuranceYou need an international plan or travel for specialist care
TransportYou live in a walkable town or the city and use taxis and busesYou need a car for a rural or beach property

How costs differ by region

  • Panama City: the highest housing costs and the widest choice of everything else. You may not need a car.
  • Pacific beaches (Coronado and nearby): moderate housing costs, but most people need a car and air conditioning.
  • Chiriquí highlands (Boquete, Volcán): mild temperatures keep electricity bills low; Boquete housing costs more than Volcán.
  • Azuero Peninsula (Pedasí, Las Tablas, Chitré): often among the more affordable regions, with a strong local culture and fewer imported goods on the shelves.
  • Bocas del Toro: island logistics make many goods more expensive, and boat transport adds up.

Ways expats keep costs down

  • Shop at local markets and fruit stands rather than only at large supermarkets.
  • Choose a home with good cross-ventilation, shade and ceiling fans.
  • Use Pensionado discounts if you qualify.
  • Rent before buying, so you learn the real costs of a town before committing.

Common questions

Is Panama cheap to live in?

Panama is cheaper than most of North America and Western Europe, but not as cheap as some neighbouring countries. Where you live, your air-conditioning use and how much imported food you buy make the biggest difference.

What is the most expensive part of living in Panama?

For most expats it is housing, especially in Panama City. On the coast and in the city, electricity for air conditioning is often the second surprise.

Where is the cheapest place to live in Panama for expats?

Smaller towns on the Azuero Peninsula and in the Chiriquí highlands outside Boquete tend to be among the more affordable, though choice of housing and services is more limited.
